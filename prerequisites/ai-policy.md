# AI policy — CSYE 7270

Fall 2026 · Read before starting graded work. This is course policy, not a numbered book chapter.

**Required viewing:** [AI Policy for Professor Bear's Courses | Using AI Responsibly in Class](https://youtu.be/8Ut0Cdl6vMw?si=9w3aEpt1ZyAR4Kiz).

## Use AI to learn, build, inspect, and improve

AI assistance is allowed and expected. This course uses **Walker, Claude Code, and Godot**, with Claude Code access assumed by the instructor. Start with [Northeastern's Claude access page](https://claude.northeastern.edu/) and resolve account access with the instructor or university support before graded work. The course does not require you to purchase API credits to complete its opening assignment.

Use the agent to help understand source, propose a bounded change, implement authorized work, investigate failures, and prepare evidence. Your job is to formulate the problem, predict likely failures, inspect the changes, play the result, and judge whether it achieves the intended experience. A generated answer, a successful render, or a passing automated check does not establish your understanding.

Follow **Predict → Build It → Use It → Ship It → Verify**. Within that learning cycle, Walker's production process is **Game brief → Build → Playtest → Inspect → Revise → Export**. Repeat checks when changes invalidate earlier evidence.

## Credit the starting point and separate contributions

Credit the starter project, source repositories, examples, assets, collaborators, and AI tools. For the first assignment, identify walker-jumpman as the starting point and explain your character and level changes. Do not submit an unchanged starter as your own contribution.

Every submission includes `SOURCES.md`. Identify which portions were AI-assisted, what each tool contributed, what you changed or rejected, and what you personally checked or decided. Include the game source and the film's script, beat sheet, visuals, and narration in that account. A source link alone does not describe your contribution.

Keep your actual attempts and learning in `FRICTIONAL.md`. AI may organize your notes, but it must not invent struggle, timestamps, tests, human feedback, or understanding.

## You must be able to explain it

You may use Claude throughout—for the game code, GDD, script, beat sheet, build, or explainer. You remain responsible for the submission. The instructor or a TA may ask you to explain any part of the code, design, video, beat sheet, or build and to connect it to what happens in Godot.

Being unable to explain your own submission reduces points under the relevant criteria. AI assistance alone is not an automatic failure. Misrepresenting authorship, sources, test results, or what was verified is an academic-integrity matter under the course and university policies.

Keep **proposed**, **implemented**, **tested**, and **human-reviewed** distinct. Label simulated examples, scripted-input captures, reconstructed interfaces, and held frames accurately. A scripted route is not a human playtest. Do not hide a broken feature or change the game solely to create misleading demonstration evidence.

## Assignments: 100 points every 10 days

Each graded assignment is worth **100 points** and is due on the **10-day cadence** shown in Canvas:

- **60:** assignment-specific implementation and explanation, including the required Brutalist Godot explainer.
- **10:** Frictional, an honest log of effort and learning.
- **10:** properly posted GitHub version matching Canvas.
- **20:** Relative Quartile, assigned after the instructor and TAs review the full comparison group.

Meeting the stated requirements earns the criterion-based 80 points; it does not guarantee the comparative 20. Relative review considers specificity, substantive improvement, demonstrated understanding, evidence, honesty about verification, usability, and professional communication. See the [assessment policy](assessment-policy.md#relative-quartile).

## Brutalist Godot explainers are required in this course

Each assignment includes an explainer made with the appropriate **Brutalist Godot skill**: gameplay walkthrough, development/code explanation, or GDD explanation. Follow the assignment's requested coverage; you are not automatically required to make three films. The [Godot explainer guide](brutalist-godot-explainers.md) explains the choices and evidence requirements.

The film must show and explain your submitted work. The script alone is not the deliverable, and narration alone does not demonstrate a mechanism. AI narration is allowed, but it cannot replace your own ability to answer questions about the work.

Production polish counts only as professional communication. A plain, precise explainer outranks a beautiful but incorrect one. Brutalist use earns no automatic relative-score bonus, and paid media services are not required. No public YouTube upload is required by this policy. Submit the film through the designated course storage and give the instructor and TAs access.

## Share the matching version safely

GitHub contains the source and documentation; designated media storage contains MP3, MP4, and files over 25 MB. Put accessible media links and revision information in the README. Give Canvas the matching source revision and required submission files. Do not post credentials, private conversations, restricted data, or assets you lack permission to redistribute.
