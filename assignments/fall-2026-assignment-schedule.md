## Conducting AI in every submission

Each assignment follows the same division of responsibility. The student states the intended experience, bounds the work and decides what evidence would count. Claude Code or another credited tool implements a scoped change. The student inspects the diff, runs checks independently, then plays, watches or listens to the result. The final decision records what to keep, revise or reject and why.

**Conducting AI** is the direction of the work: choosing the goal, sequencing tasks and deciding when the result serves the game. **Computational Skepticism** asks what an output or test actually establishes and where its evidence ends. **Irreducibly Human** keeps taste, lived experience, purpose and accountable judgment visible. A technically correct particle effect can obscure the player; a perfectly timed animation can feel wrong; passing audio tests cannot decide whether the music belongs in the scene.

Keep these distinctions in the GDD, Frictional log and required films. Claim human playtesting only when someone actually played. Automated tests and simulated input are valuable evidence about behavior, with their limits stated.
