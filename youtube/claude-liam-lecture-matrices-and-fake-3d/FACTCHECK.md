# Fact-check (Gate F): Matrices and Fake 3D

**What this is.** Every checkable claim in the narration and on screen, with its verdict and where the verdict comes from. Verdicts: PASS (checked), CORRECTED (the brief or its source said something looser or wrong and the film says the corrected thing), EXEMPT (a definition, an analogy or design advice, labelled as such), OPEN (cannot be verified yet; says why).
**Why.** Matrix code is where "it compiles" and "it is right" are furthest apart: read the columns as rows, or multiply two tables in the wrong order, and the shader still runs and shows a plausible wrong picture. Nothing here is trusted because it was said confidently.
**Found.** 77 rows: 66 PASS, 4 CORRECTED, 7 EXEMPT, 0 OPEN. Everything numeric was measured in a real Godot window, with the expected value derived independently in Python and negative controls that must fail (61 Lab checks, all PASS). Six things were learned by running rather than reading: the shader's turn is pixel-identical to Godot's own `Node2D.rotation`; a flat card at exactly a quarter turn draws nothing; reading the table as rows and writing the point on the left give the same wrong answer, (17, 39); folding the slide tables into the three-D flip product moves the pivot; with a uniform scale the order of scale and turn does not matter (it does for a non-uniform one); and `TAU`, `PI` and `E` are documented only in the Global built-ins table of each shader type page. Four rows are CORRECTED: the brief's order of the product, its "64 is the right edge", its opening line's break, and the source's "it looks like 3D".

Machine: Apple M4 Pro, macOS, Godot **4.7.2 stable (official)**, Forward+ unless a row says Compatibility. Docs: Godot 4.7 (docs.godotengine.org/en/stable), read 2026-10-13 with the session's fetch tool (excerpts in `evidence/planning-checks/docs-quotes.md`; where a docs sentence matters it was fetched twice and is also proven by a run). The source this film follows is a third-party video course on matrices and fake 3D; this session received only the brief's description of it, so rows marked BRIEF SAID or SOURCE (brief) SAID are claims taken from that description.
