# Capture plan: Matrices and Fake 3D

**What this is.** Every Godot render and screenshot the build session must record for this film: the exact shader file, the setup, how each value moves over the beat, how long, the renderer, what must be visible at the 50 % and 85 % marks, and the result already verified in a real Godot window. Nothing is recorded yet; every number below comes from the planning runs in `evidence/planning-checks/`.
**Why.** Films show only what was really done and recorded. This is the list of things to really do, written so a medium-effort session can do them without further judgment. The pictures and timings are fixed here; the build session only has to produce them and compare.
**Found.** Fifteen Godot renders (fourteen videos and one 4K still), each of a file in `lab-shaders/` that compiles and was measured; one docs screenshot (D1); **no editor frames and no interactive editor action**, so this film adds nothing to the series-level editor recording script. Every planned pose was rendered once at 640 x 360 as a planning still (`evidence/planning-checks/png/`, contact sheet `contact-sheet-planning-stills.png`) so a reviewer can look before anything is built; the geometry was also checked in the Compatibility renderer and is identical.

## Conventions for every capture

- **30 fps, 3840 x 2160**, the SubViewport native-size route of `../_godot-shaders-series/COMPONENT-SPECS.md` over a **1280 x 720 logical canvas** (its traps apply: `content_scale_size` is Window-only; Movie Maker `--resolution` is clamped; use `process_frame` plus `RenderingServer.force_draw(false)`, never `frame_post_draw`). Renderer **Forward+** (the Lab's default; Compatibility gives the same geometry, FACTCHECK `gl1` to `gl5`). Offline fixed tick: nothing here says anything about real-time frame rate. Every sweep is **scripted** and labelled "scripted sweep" on screen (`videoLabel`).
- **Stage colour** the Lab's warm light grey `#D9D5CA`; keep a mid-tone floor (no near-black fields, no flat terracotta objects: GATE T).
- **The card.** A new texture kind `card`, drawn procedurally: cream `#FAF9F5` card, a warm grey `#A8A396` border 3/128 of its width, a teal `#5FA391` arrow pointing right (shaft x 24 to 84 and y 57 to 71, head to x 108, all in 128ths) and a teal square in the top left (x and y 12 to 28). Asymmetric on purpose: the front and the mirrored back must look different. Code: `evidence/planning-checks/runner-card.gd` (`make_image("card", n)`; it draws crisp at any size).
- **Units.** The card is drawn at **1024 px** (U = 8 times the 128 px card of the planning runs) so it stays sharp at 4K, shown at node scale **k / 8** (k = 2.0 unless a row says otherwise: a card 256 logical px wide). The shaders work in the texture's own pixels, so **every pixel-valued uniform below is listed in card pixels and must be set multiplied by U** (`slide` (100, 0) becomes (800, 0); `pivot` (-64, 0) becomes (-512, 0)). The `lab-shaders/` files are never edited; their defaults are overridden. Angle and flip values are not scaled. Scale-invariance was measured (`scale-invariance-summary.txt`): identical bounding boxes at 128 px and at 1024 px with U = 8.
- **Markers drawn by the scene.** An *origin cross* (a small grey cross, 24 logical px, at the node's origin) where a row says so; a *hinge dot* (terracotta `#D97757`, radius 6 logical px, a filled circle, never text) at the pivot, placed at the sprite's position plus the pivot times the node scale. Readouts, rings and captions are drawn by `ShaderLab`, not by the scene.
- **Keyframes** are fractions of the beat (the harness converts them with `--length`), listed as breakpoints and held to the end; each moving segment is eased in-out. `--animate` is piecewise linear, so write the eased breakpoints with a few lines of Python (a smoothstep sampled every 1/30 s between the two listed points) or ease inside the scene script; a vec2 uniform takes `x,y`. Example for C01: `--animate flip:0:0,0.10:0,0.38:0.5,0.62:0.5,0.80:1.0,1.0:1.0` with the eased points added.
- **Length.** Record each video at the length given (the estimated narration time plus 15 %, rounded up), then, once the audio is measured, compare: if a beat's measured audio differs from its capture by more than 10 %, re-record that capture at the measured length (the keyframes are fractions, so only the length changes). Never speed up or slow down a render.
- **Output** to `brutalist.art/runtime/remotion/public/godot-shaders/` under the names in `lanes/` (all start `matrices-`).

## What the harness needs for this film

`../_godot-shaders-series/COMPONENT-SPECS.md` already lists what the capture harness does: `--animate` schedules (piecewise linear, holds are breakpoints), `--scene` (a scene script builds the captured content), `--material-at`, stills as PNG, keyframes as fractions of the clip with `--length`. This film uses exactly those, plus one scene script of its own:

1. **A card scene script** (`--scene`), built from `evidence/planning-checks/runner-card.gd`: the `card` texture drawn at 1024 px (`make_image("card", n)`), the `Sprite2D` at node scale k / 8 with the film's shader, the unit factor U = 8 applied to pixel-valued uniforms, the origin cross and the terracotta hinge dot where a row says so. C06 is the two-panel variant of the same script (two 640 x 720 panels, one card each; the Lines film's `../claude-liam-lecture-lines-and-shine/evidence/planning-checks/two_panels.gd` is the precedent for panels side by side).
2. **Eased breakpoints** for every moving segment (see "Keyframes" above).
3. **`--material-at`** for C02 (the shader assigned at 60 % of the beat) and a **4K still** for C11, both in the spec.

Nothing here needs `--steps`, `--call-at`, `--time-origin` or `--keys`: no input is scripted and no `TIME` is read.
