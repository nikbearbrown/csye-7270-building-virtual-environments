# Fact-check (Gate F): Outlines

**What this is.** One row for every checkable claim the film speaks or shows: each with a verdict, the source or run that settles it, and what the film does about it. Verdicts are PASS, CORRECTED (a claim in the brief or its third-party source that was loose or wrong), EXEMPT (a definition or a design judgment, said so) and OPEN (not verifiable now).
**Why.** Bear's rule: every factual claim is verified before it ships, and a shader is where "it compiles" and "it looks right" are furthest apart. So each Godot behavior here was either found in the Godot 4.7 documentation (read 2026-10-13) or run, and the run is in `evidence/planning-checks/`.
**Found.** 81 rows: 68 PASS, 6 CORRECTED, 7 EXEMPT, 0 OPEN. The six corrections: the brief's subtraction form of the line color (F14), "ifs are slow", "animated sprites just work", "gaps on diagonals with eight samples", the deprecated way to create the padded image, and `filter_nearest` for the node's own texture. Four more things the brief did not mention were found by running: a shader paints only inside its rectangle, sprite sheets leak into the next frame, soft edges and the default linear filter break the line, and a shader that assigns COLOR from the texture drops modulate.

How to read the source column: Lab A1 and the like are sections of `evidence/planning-checks/lab-summary.txt` (the Shader Lab batch, scale 1); scene C13 and the like are lines of `scenes-summary.txt` (the capture specs measured at the film's own geometry, 1280 x 720 canvas, sprites at whole-number scales, outline widths in canvas pixels); run-c, run-j and the like are the logs `run-<letter>-verify_<name>.log`; D1, K1, B1 and the like are sections of the matching `*-summary.txt`. The docs are the stable Godot documentation on 2026-10-13, which matches the installed 4.7.2. Third-party sources are paraphrased, never quoted (SOURCES.md).
