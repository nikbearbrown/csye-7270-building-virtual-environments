## claude-liam-lecture-light-shaders-slate

Light shaders learn what the light function does and what godo hands it, then use it to build a sweeping lighthouse beam and a ring that spreads from a lamp. Hello, this is Liam in Forbear. Lighting in a 2D game feels built in, you place a light and godo does the rest. Most people think that is where your control ends, it is not. A shader can replace it. Six terms, a shader is a small program the graphics card runs to decide how something is drawn. A pixel is one dot of the picture. A texture is an image, a shader can read. The canvas is the surface 2D things are drawn on. A vector is a bundle of 2 to 4 numbers, and a mask is a number from 0 to 1 for each pixel, saying how much of something to show. In a 2D game, a light is a soft glow. Same scene, same light, and one function later, that glow is a beam sweeping the ground. The function is called light, and before this film ends, you will have written it. Godo has two kinds of 2D light. A point light shines out from one spot, like a lamp. A directional light shines the same way everywhere, like the sun. This film works mostly with the lamp. A light needs a dim scene to show against. A 2D scene starts fully lit, so a light can only brighten what is already bright. Add a canvas modulate node, which tints everything and set it dim. That tint is the ambient light. A soft glow is a texture. Godo's docks give the recipe. Give the light a gradient texture, set its fill to radial, and run the gradient from opaque white to transparent white. The transparent part becomes the soft edge. A point light has a few settings. Its texture sets its shape. Its scale sets how far it reaches. Color tints it, and energy is how strong it is. Change each one, and the same lamp lights the same ground differently. Lights add two lamps overlapping, make the overlap brighter, and three make it brighter still. Underneath the dim ambient stays put, whether a light reaches a pixel or not. Godo's rule measured. The pixel is the surface color times the total of the ambient in every light. A gray surface of one half, an ambient of point two, two lamps of point four each. The pixel reads one half. A 2D shader can have three functions. Vertex places corners and fragment colors each pixel. The third light runs for every pixel in every light that reaches it and asks, how much does this lamp add here? Add a function called light to a shader, and it replaces Godo's default lighting for that sprite, even an empty one. The lamp still lights the blob, but the ground ignores it. The function belongs to the sprite, not to the lamp. The smallest light function returns one flat answer, point two gray. Add a lamp in this card, brightens by point two. Add another, and it brightens again. Light runs once for each lamp, and Godo adds the answers. Inside light, Godo hands you built tins, values written in capital letters. Color is this pixel's own color, and UV is its address. Light color and light energy describe the lamp. You hand back one value also called light, what this lamp adds. Light runs last. Fragment runs first and sets the pixels color, which arrives in light as the built in color. Then light runs once for each lamp, and Godo adds all the answers to the dim ambient look. That sum is the pixel. Rebuild Godo's default. The color is the surface times the lamp's color times its energy. The fourth number alpha passes straight through. Rendered both ways, the same scene differs in no pixel at all. The fourth number is the lamp's soft edge, the texture fades from one to zero. Force it to one, and every pixel the texture covers is lit at full strength. The soft glow becomes a hard square. Now reshape it, round the fade down to quarters, and the glow becomes bands like cell shading. Same lamp, same ground. Only the light function changed how the light fades. Or let time shape it. Multiply the light by the absolute value of a sign of time in the lamp breathes. No animation player, no game code, the shader does it every frame. One more switch, put render mode unshaded at the top, and the sprite ignores every light and the ambient tint too. That is right for the lamp's own bulb. Otherwise it goes dim with the room. One caution, this copy matches the default shadows included, but the default also reads normal maps, images that say which way each pixel faces, and this copy ignores them. The docs show the extra term that fixes it. Change one thing at a time. Now direction, light direction is a vector, an arrow from this pixel toward the lamp always length one. A pixel left of the lamp gets an arrow pointing right. A pixel above it gets an arrow pointing down. To see a number use it as brightness, take the arrow's x number from minus 1 to 1 and squeeze it into 0 to 1, multiply the lamp by it, left of the lamp full strength, right of the lamp nothing. Turn that number into a mask with step. Step gives 0 below an edge, and one at or above it. Use an edge of 0, multiply the light by the result, and only the left half of the lamp stays on. Take the absolute value in both sides count. Raise the edge close to 1, and only pixels where the arrow is nearly sideways survive. That leaves a wedge on the left and a wedge on the right. A directional light is different. Its arrow is the same for every pixel on screen, and its position is always 0, so a direction mask lights everything or nothing, for a beam use a point light. To aim a beam you need an arrow of your own. Co-sign and sign of an angle make one, always length one. Turn the angle and the arrow turns with it. Then compare two arrows with the dot product. It is one when they point the same way, zero at right angles, and minus one when they point opposite ways, keep the pixels where it is close to 1. Let time turn the angle and the wedge turns with it, a light house. Two details, our arrow points at the lamp so flip its sign, and because why points down a growing angle turns the beam clockwise. Make the cut off the number inside step a uniform, a dial your game sets from outside. Lower it in the beam widens, raise it in the beam narrows. Swap step for smooth step which ramps between two edges instead of cutting, and the edge of the beam goes soft. Same lamp, same beam, a better look. At a second lamp and light runs again for it with its own arrows. Two beams from one shader, each starting at its own lamp, each in its own color, and no extra code. A beam exists only where a lamp reaches. Outside the lamp's texture, Godot never calls light at all. For a longer beam, scale the lamp not the shader. Two more numbers, light position is where the lamp is, and light vertex is where this pixel is. Both are in screen space. Pixels of the window, counted from the top left with Y growing downward. Subtract the lamp's position from the pixels, and you get an arrow from the lamp to this pixel. Its length is the distance between them in pixels. That is what the length function measures. Use that distance as brightness, nothing at the lamp a little more with every step away. Now every pixel knows exactly how far it is from the light. Step the distance against a radius and the ground splits into, inside and outside. Do it for two radii, subtract one from the other, and what is left is the band between them. A ring. Put it together in the lamp lights only a band of ground. The distance two steps, one subtraction, multiplied into the light. The radius and the thickness are uniforms, so the game can change both. Now the game drives it, a tween moves a value smoothly over time. Set the radius to zero from code, then tween it out to the edge of the lamp, and a ring spreads from the light. This run is scripted, not player input. One warning, these are window pixels, so the ring does not follow your world. Zoom the camera in, and the ground doubles in size, but a ring of 450 pixels stays 450 pixels. One more tool mod, it counts up to a number, then starts over. Mod the pixels x by 128, keep the first 16, and you get stripes. They belong to the screen, not the sprite, slide the ground, and they stay put. Do the same for y, and join the two with max. You get a grid of light, lines 16 pixels wide, every 128 pixels, drawn only where the lamp reaches. So when does a job belong in light? When it depends on a lamp, its direction, its distance, its color. A pattern on the surface alone belongs in fragment, and a wobble in the corners in vertex, ask where the effect comes from. Let's recap with Claude, a pixel is its surface times the total of the ambient and every light. Writing light replaces the default, and you can rebuild it exactly. Light direction is an arrow from the pixel toward the lamp. Aim an arrow, compare it with a dot, and a mask becomes a beam. Light position and light vertex give a distance, and distance makes rings. Those positions are window pixels, so they ignore your camera zoom. Return, paste this into Claude, write a go-do canvas item shader with a light function for sprite. A narrow beam sweeps clockwise around the lamp once every two seconds. Use the built in light direction in the built in time, and keep the lamp soft edge, color, and energy.
