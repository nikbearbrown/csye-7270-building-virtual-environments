In the shader, an angle goes in, and two numbers come out that say where the moon is. Those numbers come from a triangle. Start with a triangle that has a right angle in one corner. Its longest side is the hypotenuse. Now pick another corner. The side across from it is the opposite side. The side touching it is the adjacent side. Here is the useful part, fix the angle and the ratio of any two side stays fixed, whatever the size. At 30 degrees, the opposite side is always half the hypotenuse, at 50, about 0.77 of it. Those ratios have names. Opposite over hypotenuse is the sign. Adjacent over hypotenuse is the cosine. Opposite over adjacent is the tangent. The memory trick is SOH, C-A-H-T-O-A. So sign and cosine are lookups, hand one an angle, and it hands back a ratio. Sign of 30 degrees is one half. Cosine of 30 degrees is about 0.87. An angle goes in, a number comes out.
