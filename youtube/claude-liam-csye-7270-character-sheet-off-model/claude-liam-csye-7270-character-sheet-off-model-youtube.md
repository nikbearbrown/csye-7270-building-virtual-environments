# YouTube — On Model on Paper, Off Model on Film

## Title

The Character Sheet Was On Model. The AI Film Wasn't.

Alternate: On Model on Paper, Off Model on Film: What an AI Video Did to Our Character Sheet

## Description

We made two character sheets for Dorothy: a five-view full-body turnaround and a five-angle head turnaround. Both are on model: one costume, one face, ten views. Then we gave them to an AI video model and got a three-minute film.

Liam, in for Bear, judges the sheets, plays the film exactly as it was generated, music and all, then puts the sheet beside frames from the film. The costume mostly holds. The face doesn't: it changes from shot to shot, the hair turns auburn, and the braids move from the nape to the crown. Toto, who never had a sheet, drifts even further.

The lesson for game and film makers: a character sheet is the spec you check against. It won't keep an AI generator on model by itself. It's how you catch the drift, not how you prevent it.

Made for CSYE 7270, Building Virtual Environments (Fall 2026), as a companion to Assignment 2, "Generate Art, Sound, and Music for Your Game." That assignment asks students to make the character sheet; rendering comes later.

Your turn: paste your own character sheet into Claude and ask it to list the five features most likely to drift when a video model animates your character, with a one-line check for each, so you can score any frame on model or off model.
