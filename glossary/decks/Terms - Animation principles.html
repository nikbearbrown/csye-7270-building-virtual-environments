Computed

The speed curve

Easing from first principles

Source: Chapter 10, “Between keys”; glossary, Animation (Easing); Riot ep 6 (ease, spacing).

Formulas: ease-in p = t squared; ease-out p = 1 - (1-t) squared; ease-in-out p = 3t squared - 2t cubed. Speeds are the numerical derivative. Ease-in-out has no glossary row; it is a standard name (CSS and Godot ease curves). Chapter 10 explains the per-key transition value, where 1 is linear.

Parts that keep going

Source: The Illusion of Life, Thomas and Johnston: the 12 principles; named in; Riot series ep 6 Character Animation.

Follow-through, overlap and secondary motion are from the 12 principles the Riot video names but does not teach; tagged outside source. Tail: a damped spring (omega 14, damping ratio 0.22) integrated at 60 fps; hair: follow-the-leader chain with fixed segment length. Both precomputed at load.

Skeleton, bone, rest pose and weights: Chapter 10 "Skeletons and IK" (Skeleton2D, painted weights, rest pose). Rig is a glossary row. Linear blend skinning is computed per vertex: position = (1 - w) × upper-bone position + w × forearm-bone position, with w a smoothstep across 140 px at the elbow.

Computed

Posing a limb

FK, IK and retargeting

Source: Chapter 10, “Skeletons and IK”; glossary, Animation (FK / IK) and Animation foundations (Retargeting).

IK is explained in Chapter 10 (Skeletons and IK; the Unity two-bone IK passage says the tip of a limb reaches a target). FK and retargeting are glossary only. Two-bone IK uses the law of cosines: cos(elbow) = (d squared - L1 squared - L2 squared) / (2 L1 L2). Retargeting here scales the target by the ratio of total reach.
