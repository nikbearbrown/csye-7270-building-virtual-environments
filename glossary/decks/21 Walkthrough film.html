Walkthrough film, central idea, beat, beat sheet

Source: Chapter 15, “The final Brutalist film”; Assignments 8 and 10; building-walkthrough SKILL.md and walkthrough-craft.md.

The example film (Walker Jumpman level 1) and its beat lengths are invented for illustration. Total is the sum of the beat lengths. Walkthrough film is explained in Chapter 15, “The final Brutalist film”; the beat sheet and central idea come from the toolkit skill and Assignment 8.

Plan it

Storyboard, script, voice-over, pacing

Source: building-walkthrough SKILL.md and walkthrough-craft.md (pace section); Assignment 8 beat sheet.

Speech time is words divided by 2.4 words a second, the toolkit gate’s estimate; once real audio exists its measured length replaces it. Word counts are computed from the example lines. Beat lengths are illustrative.

Route it

Shot, route, waypoint, establishing shot

Source: building-walkthrough SKILL.md and walkthrough-craft.md; glossary, Game AI section (Waypoint).

Leg lengths are computed from the waypoint coordinates; time uses 1.0 m/s, inside the walking-speed default of 0.7 to 1.2. Landmarks counted by containment in each frame rectangle. Order of the sections follows rule 2 in walkthrough-craft.md.

Computed

Route it

Camera move and route check

Source: building-walkthrough SKILL.md and walkthrough-craft.md, rule 5 and 6; 18 Camera deck; glossary, Camera section.

Pan rate, dolly speed and orbit speed are computed from stated inputs. Limits (1.5 m/s, 30°/s, 0.5°, 1.6 m ±0.10) are the toolkit’s starting points. The sine ease has peak = average × π/2. Measured values in the table are illustrative.

Record it

Real-time capture, render, fixed frame rate, deterministic capture

Source: godot-waikthrough SKILL.md and capture-and-coverage.md; Chapter 0, headless runs and --fixed-fps; glossary, Toolchain.

Frame times are a seeded simulation (about one frame in five takes 1.6 to 3.2 times the budget). Render time uses an illustrative 4 s a frame. Movie Maker is offline rendering and is not evidence of real-time fps, per the capture reference.

Frame rate, frame count, resolution, aspect ratio

Source: glossary, 2D art and sound assets (Resolution, Aspect ratio) and Animation (Frame rate); godot-waikthrough SKILL.md and capture-and-coverage.md.

Pixel counts and ratios are computed from the sizes shown. The 3840×2160 native capture requirement is from the walkthrough skill; resolution and aspect ratio are glossary rows.

Cut, b-roll and title card

Source: outside source, general film vocabulary (not fetched); godot-waikthrough SKILL.md (regular outro).

Overlap arithmetic is computed. Cut and b-roll are general film vocabulary from an outside source; no page was fetched. The title card and the regular outro card are described in the godot-waikthrough skill.

Safe margins, captions, decibel, loudness

Source: ai-explainer SKILL.md, title-safe inset; a course film’s LOUDNESS.md; glossary, Decibel and dBFS; godot-waikthrough SKILL.md.

Pixel insets and decibel values are computed. The −24.55 LUFS and −2.96 dBTP are the measured numbers in LOUDNESS.md of one course lecture film; the window −28 to −18 and ceiling −1.0 are that gate’s settings.
