# RIFF.md — the commentary, and what it is allowed to say

Liam, in for Bear. Teardown register. The riff is on **inspected output**, never
on imagined play.

## The three things worth saying out loud

**1. A string is testable; a texture is not.** `pose_key()` could have returned
a `Texture2D` and the code would read the same. Returning a string is what lets
a headless check drive eight states and read the answer back. The riff is that
the testable choice and the obvious choice were different, and the testable one
cost a dictionary lookup.

**2. The rim argument is arithmetic, not taste.** "It looked wrong" is not a
reason anyone can check. "A four-sided rim costs a part 2 px of width, so a
3 px arm keeps 1 px of armour" is. The film says the second and shows the
measurement — 6.3 % to 24.2 % — because that is the form in which a reader can
disagree.

**3. The blade is the honest cost.** In a film about generated art, the
character's most characteristic object is deliberately not generated. The riff
does not hide that trade; it names it, because the alternative is a silent
coupling between a picture and a hitbox that no existing check would catch.

## What the riff refuses to do

- **Claim the footage proves the art is right.** It shows motion and the log
  shows which pose was live. Neither says the drawing is correct, and
  `TEST-REPORT.md` §6 says so first.
- **Call the sound effects generated.** They are arithmetic. Said in B01, in
  B06's notes, and in B12.
- **Present a reconstruction as a recording.** The composer prompt is labelled
  on screen and in the first ten seconds of narration.
- **Round a number up.** 124.018 BPM, not 124. 3.8 × 10⁻⁶ px, not "zero".
  35 ticks, not "about half a second" alone.
- **Imply an import workflow this project does not use.** The 15 `.import`
  sidecars are excluded with a reason: the game loads every asset by absolute
  path and never reads them.

## The one joke

There isn't one. The register is Teardown and the subject is a student
assignment with a placeholder in it; a laugh at this material would be at the
work's expense.
