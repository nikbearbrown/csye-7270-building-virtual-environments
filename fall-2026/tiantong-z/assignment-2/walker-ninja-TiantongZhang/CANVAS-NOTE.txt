WHAT THIS ASSIGNMENT ADDED

  This continues the Assignment 1 build rather than starting a new game. The
  slice is the same 3072 px level with the same eight movement values; what
  changed is what it looks like and what it sounds like.

  Generated music (1 asset). Suno v6-mini, prompt recorded verbatim as Suno's
  own library shows it. Eight tracks across two directions, every one
  auditioned in the browser, one chosen. Tempo was recovered FROM THE AUDIO
  rather than taken from the prompt - spectral-flux onset envelope,
  autocorrelated, refined by a comb search over period and phase: 124.018 BPM,
  bar 1.9352 s. Three candidate loops were cut at bar boundaries and scored for
  spectral continuity across the splice; the chosen one is 16 bars (30.9632 s)
  from 29.722 s with a 20 ms equal-power crossfade at the wrap. Its two seams
  measure at the 10th and 8th percentile of the track's own frame-to-frame
  spectral change - the wrap changes the spectrum less than 90% of ordinary
  moments in the music do.

  Generated art (9 assets). A character reference plus eight poses, one per
  state the engine can already distinguish: idle, run, rising, falling, dash,
  attack wind-up, attack live, death. Imported by scripts/import_pose.py into
  32x32 cells anchored at (20,30), snapped to an eight-colour palette, and
  wired into player.gd. The sprite the player sees is generated art; the
  dungeon, the HUD, the enemies and the traps are still original vector
  drawing in code.

  The BLADE is deliberately still drawn in code. It comes from ATTACK_PIVOT,
  attack_angle() and attack_reach - the same three numbers the kill hitbox is
  built from - so the poses were prompted with "empty hand, NO weapon". A blade
  baked into a sprite would stop tracking the hitbox the moment anyone touched
  the tuning and nothing would say so: the two attack-hitbox-on-the-blade
  checks would still pass, because they measure the hitbox against the numbers
  rather than against the picture.

  A dungeon setting. Stone wall, staggered mortar courses, blind arches, wall
  torches, torchlit platform edges. Original vector drawing. This inverted the
  character's whole contrast situation and the fix shipped with it: the 1 px
  rim went from dark to light (steel_edge, 7.31:1 against the wall where the
  old dark outline was 1.14:1), and the floor is now read from its lit top
  edge at 6.96:1 rather than from its body at 1.43:1.

  A death pose. Prone, face down, head the way the character was travelling -
  the only pose in the game wider than it is tall, roughly 26x9 against 18x28
  standing, which is why it reads without a pixel of detail being legible.

  A 4:54 film, built by the Brutalist toolkit's godot-gamedev pipeline. Fifteen
  beats at 3840x2160. Five of them are source excerpts pulled from the hashed
  files by a ledger that refuses to build if the displayed text, the beat's
  props and the file on disk disagree; each is followed by the result those
  lines produce, bound to it by hash. Three beats are real play - native 4K
  Godot Movie Maker output driven through real InputEvents by a capture script
  that asserts its own outcome, so a finished render is not the claim. The
  narration is Kokoro-82M (local, Apache-2.0, voice am_onyx) and every cue
  position is a fraction of the measured audio rather than of an estimate.
  youtube/claude-liam-walker-ninja-gamedev/ holds the shotlist, the fact-check
  table with one row per spoken number, and the sources.

  Two defects in it were found by rendering the master and looking at frames,
  which nothing in the pipeline checks. One is fixed: the two evidence images
  did not fill the panel they render in, and are composed to its measured
  1529x987 now. One is not, and is declared in that folder's SHOTLIST.md - the
  two result beats render an empty code panel across the left 45% of the frame,
  because the shared component's asset layout draws {code}{image} and their
  code must stay empty. Fixing it meant either breaking the teaching contract
  or extending a component in a toolkit I do not own, the day before the
  deadline.

  Audio wiring. A 16-bar loop on its own bus that survives every retry without
  restarting, four sound effects on a second bus, a mute key on 0 that mutes
  the Master bus, and a hard limiter on Master because the music and the trap
  sound sum to 1.512 when their peaks align.

KNOWN LIMITATIONS, DECLARED

  1. THE FOUR SOUND EFFECTS ARE NOT GENERATED. They are synthesised by
     arithmetic in scripts/synth_placeholder_sfx.py - closed-form waveforms and
     one seeded noise array - and they do not satisfy this assignment's
     "generate sound" requirement. ASSET-LOG.md opens its sound-effects section
     with that sentence and godot/assets/README.md has a "Generated?" column
     that reads "no" four times.

     They exist because CONCEPT.md revision 1.1 predicted on 2026-10-01 that a
     124 bpm bed with pulsing bass would mask the trap's 15-frame warning, and
     that check is perceptual: it cannot be automated and it cannot be run
     against silence either. With placeholders in, it runs now and runs again
     against the generated versions, comparable instead of sequential.
     Replacement is file-for-file; no trigger, assertion, level or balance
     changes.

  2. THE ART WAS GENERATED ON A TOOL THAT EXPOSES NO SEED, which breaks this
     project's own requirement. CHARACTER-SHEET.md section 8 carried "the
     generator must record a seed" as a preference from the start, and a
     correction dated 2026-10-04 made it binding after Suno turned out to
     expose none - which left the music reproducible as a request and not as an
     output. The art has the same gap. It is a deliberate departure taken after
     five generations on a tool that DOES record a seed failed to produce a
     usable frame, all five logged with what each failed on. The rule was
     right; it lost to a worse problem, and ASSET-LOG.md says so rather than
     switching quietly.

     Model and version: GPT-6, as I reported it. Its own licence terms are
     the one provenance question still open in this project.

  3. TWO EDITS ARE APPLIED TO EVERY GENERATED POSE by scripts/import_pose.py,
     and they are edits to generated assets rather than properties of the
     generation:
       - the directional rim. The reference came back with a near-black
         outline, which snaps to `shade` at 1.14:1 against the dungeon wall -
         nothing at all. The importer adds 1 px of steel_edge toward the back
         and up. Measured at real game size this takes the fraction of the
         figure reading at 3:1 or better against the wall from 6.3% to 24.2%;
         the code-drawn character measures 9.1% on the same test.
       - restoring the visor through the downscale. The slit is 1-2 px tall at
         a 28 px figure height, an area resample averages it into the navy helm
         and the result snaps to `plate`: 0 visor pixels in seven of the first
         eight imports. 6-9 px restored per pose.
     What the model produced is a slit in a 1254 px image. What the importer
     does is keep it alive at 28.

  4. THE MUSIC IS NON-COMMERCIAL USE ONLY, and this project has to stay
     non-commercial while it is in it. Suno's Help Center puts the test in one
     sentence - "was I subscribed when I made the song?" - and answers it: a
     Pro or Premier subscriber at creation time owns the song; on the free
     Basic tier Suno retains ownership and you may use the song for
     non-commercial purposes.

     Ownership turns on the plan AT CREATION TIME, not on how the file was
     later obtained. These tracks were generated on 2026-10-01 on the free
     tier with no Pro or Premier subscription at any point; the
     single-download pack bought afterwards purchased a download, not a
     licence upgrade. So Suno owns MUS-B-02 and a graded course submission is
     inside the non-commercial grant - but if this slice is ever sold,
     monetised or commercially released, the music has to be replaced first. A
     film of it posted publicly and unmonetised is still non-commercial use.

     Scope of what I checked: the Help Center article, read 2026-10-06. The
     Terms of Service themselves I did not read in full and I could not find
     the pack's receipt. Nothing above rests on either.

     Also for the record: eight tracks were generated on the free tier and
     ZERO downloaded there. The free allowance was reported exhausted because
     Suno moved to seven lifetime downloads on 2026-09-03 and applied it
     retroactively, so downloads made on this account long before this course
     count against it. The paywall was not circumvented and nothing was
     screen-recorded.

  5. THE TEST SUITE DOES NOT ASSERT WHAT THE PLAYER CHARACTER LOOKS LIKE. All
     113 checks are behavioural - where the hitbox is, whether the trap is
     drawn, which tick a sound fired on. Two real art defects passed all of
     them while being obviously wrong on screen: an air pose that put a boot
     inside the floor (y is negative upward and a leg lift was adding instead
     of subtracting), and a rim that left 1 px of armour inside a 3 px arm so
     the limbs rendered as grey pipework.

     This is not going to be fixed with a pixel-diff of the player: that test
     fails on every intentional art change and gets deleted within a week. The
     honest position is that capture_character.gd plus build_char_sheet.py ARE
     the check - they assert the state of each of 11 captured frames, and a
     human has to look at the frames. That is a weaker guarantee than the rest
     of the report and nothing here implies otherwise.

  6. THE PLACEHOLDER DEATH REACTION IS STILL IN PLACE. godot/assets/
