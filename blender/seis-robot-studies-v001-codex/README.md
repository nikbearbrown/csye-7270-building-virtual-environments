# SEIS AI Lab robot, design studies v001: compact and slim

*Two rigged white-box-head robot mascots for the SEIS AI Lab, built from primitives in Blender. A design study, not a final model.*

`2 models · 135 meshes each · 51,536 (compact) and 51,984 (slim) triangles · 7 materials in the GLB · 17 bones each · 1.68 m (compact) and 2.08 m (slim) tall with antenna · Blender 5.1.2`

Two takes on the same mascot: a compact, boxy lead design (large rectangular head, short legs, broad feet) and a slimmer, long-legged comparison. Both have a white box head with two raised black oval eyes and a thin black waveform mouth, a red 6 modelled as real mesh on the chest, white/gray/black parts with red accents, a 17-bone forward-kinematics skeleton and two mouth shape keys (Speaking, Emphasis). They are editable models made from primitives; the supplied drawing's distressed ink texture was not reproduced. This is a design study, not a final model. The source says both still need Bear's aesthetic review.

## Compare with references

### Supplied drawing vs compact (three-quarter)

Left: the supplied approved-waveform drawing. Right: the compact render. The head box, oval eyes, waveform mouth, red 6 and red-dot joints are carried over. It falls short on everything that gives the drawing life: the drawing's torso is a rounded block with a lean body and long legs, the compact is a squat figure with a huge head; the drawing has rounded shoes and an open gesturing hand, the render has slab feet and a stiff relaxed arm; the ink-wear texture is absent. Likeness: recognisably the same idea, but a clean toy version, and the proportions are the biggest miss.

<img src="compare/web/waveform-ref_vs_compact-three-quarter.jpg" width="720" alt="Supplied drawing vs compact (three-quarter)">

### Supplied drawing vs slim (three-quarter)

Left: the same supplied drawing. Right: the slim render. Limb length and overall height are nearer the drawing than in the compact model. Still missing: the rounded torso, the rounded shoes, the ink texture and the raised hand. The slim legs and arms are straighter and more uniform than the drawing's articulated joints. Likeness: closer in proportion than compact, still a simplified primitive version.

### Supplied boxy reference vs compact (bent pose)

Left: the supplied boxy-body reference (a dark, stylised image of a tilted box head on a small cylindrical body). Right: the compact bent-pose render. The tilted box head, small body and two-antenna idea match loosely; the reference has two antennas, a camera-lens fitting on the head side, round chunky torso and arm cylinders and a worn black finish. ## How to import into Blender

- **`.blend`:** open it in Blender 4.2 or later (File > Open). Saved by Blender 5.1.2; it has never been opened in Blender 4.2, so 4.2 compatibility is untested (use Blender 5.1 or later to match the build).
- **`.glb`:** in Blender, File > Import > glTF 2.0 (.glb/.gltf), or drop it into a game engine or a web viewer.

## Built by

- **Builder:** Codex (OpenAI). Builder identified from Codex session logs on this machine dated 2026-10-18, not from a label in the folder; no product or model version is stated in the files.
- **Method:** Scripted: one Blender Python script (scripts/build_robot.py) builds either variant from primitives in a fresh background Blender. No sculpting and no AI image-to-3D. The source lists no numbered render-versus-reference rounds; it records one headless build per variant, a render pass, and mechanical checks. The compare images in this folder were made afterwards for this page, by eye.
- **Tools:** Blender 5.1.2 (confirmed by my scene count); Khronos glTF Blender I/O v5.1.20; the source's own validator (zero errors, zero warnings, both variants); Godot 4.7.2 import test recorded by the source (both pass). The source says the guide named Blender 5.2 LTS but 5.1.2 was what was installed.
- **Build date:** 2026-10-17 (file times 16:21 to 16:25 and START-HERE.txt; the folder's own note says October 17, 2026)
- **Human review:** Professor Bear: pending. The source START-HERE.txt says: "these first design studies still need Bear's aesthetic review."

## Honest limitations

- This is a design study, not a final model.
- The compact is the source's "lead design" label, not a human decision recorded in the folder.
- Proportions differ from the supplied drawing (huge head on a squat body in the compact); the drawing's distressed ink texture, rounded shoes and open gesturing hand are not reproduced.
- Fingers are rigid parts of each hand, not rigged individually. No IK, control rig, walk cycle or animation; the rig is simple FK with every part fully weighted to one bone.
- The mouth uses two discrete shape-key controls; no speech sync, no sound is attached.
- Counts: my count_scene run on the .blend files reports 136 mesh objects and 51,538 / 51,986 triangles, which is the source's 135 meshes and 51,536 / 51,984 triangles plus a 2-triangle floor plane; the .blend also shows 8 materials against 7 in the GLB (the extra is presentation-only).
- Height figures (1.6815 m and 2.0765 m with antenna) come from the source's own audit; my count cannot give them because of the large floor plane.
- Verification of Godot import and the GLB round-trip is as recorded by the source; I did not re-run it.
