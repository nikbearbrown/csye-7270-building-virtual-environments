# Crystal Palace — key architectural facts

Distilled from the seed article ([The Crystal Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Crystal_Palace),
plain-text copy in `data/seed-article.txt`) and corroborating sources below. Numbers are quoted
from the article; see the "Disputed numbers" note for the one inconsistency.

## Identity
- The Crystal Palace: a cast-iron and plate-glass exhibition building, originally erected in
  Hyde Park, London, to house the **Great Exhibition of the Works of Industry of All Nations**
  (1 May – 15 October 1851), the first World's Fair.
- After the exhibition it was dismantled, moved, and rebuilt at Sydenham Hill (Penge Place),
  where it stood from **June 1854** until its destruction by fire in **November 1936**.

## Architect and builders
- **Architect: Joseph Paxton** — then head gardener to the Duke of Devonshire at Chatsworth;
  famed for large glasshouses (Chatsworth's Lily House). Knighted by Queen Victoria for the work.
- Construction partner: civil engineering contractor **Fox, Henderson and Co** (director
  Charles Fox, also knighted). Consulting engineer: **William Cubitt**.
- Paxton's first concept sketch was doodled on pink blotting paper on 11 June 1850 during a
  Midland Railway board meeting — that sheet survives and is now in the V&A (item O18167;
  see `images/va-paxton-sketches/`). He won the commission in July 1850 with under eight
  months to manufacture and assemble the building.

## Dimensions and structure
- Ground plan (original Hyde Park form): **1,848 ft × 456 ft (563 m × 139 m)** — a grid
  77 modules long by 19 modules wide. Each module was self-supporting, so the building could
  theoretically be extended indefinitely.
- Interior height 128 ft (39 m); central transept covered by a 72 ft-wide (22 m)
  barrel-vaulted roof, 168 ft (51 m) high at the top of the arch. Most of the roof was flat
  profile. Three tiers of galleries, all open to the central transept.
- **Materials:** modular cast-iron frame, wooden sash bars, plate glass. Reportedly
  ~3,300 cast-iron columns, ~2,200 iron girders, ~900,000 sq ft of glass
  (figures vary by source; see below). Ironwork was painted to look like stone in some areas.
- The transept was a late addition forced by Parliament's requirement to preserve the large
  Hyde Park elms on the site — Paxton's barrel-vaulted transept enclosed the trees, one of
  the building's most famous design gestures.

## Disputed numbers (honest note)
- The article's lede states the building was **1,851 feet (564 m) long**; the design section
  measures the ground plan at **1,848 feet (563 m) by 456 feet (139 m)**. The 1,848 ft figure
  is the measured plan (77 × 24-ft modules); the 1,851 ft figure appears to conflate the
  year of the exhibition with the length. Use 1,848 ft / 563 m as the surveyed dimension.

## Sydenham era
- Rebuilt 1852–54 at Sydenham Hill by the Crystal Palace Company; reopened June 1854 with
  added courts (Egyptian, Greek, Roman, Renaissance, etc.), fountains, and winter gardens.
- Hosted exhibitions, concerts, football cup finals (FA Cup finals 1895–1914), and — from
  1932 — the Imperial War Museum's precursor displays. Destroyed by fire on **30 November
  1936**; the ruins' foundations and terraces survive in Crystal Palace Park.
